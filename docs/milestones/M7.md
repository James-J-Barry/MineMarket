# M7: Bonds (Bond Desk, Treasury and Corporate Bonds, the central rate, defaults; Digital Record Keeping)

Source: `docs/design.md` → progression table (Tier 5), "Item and block catalog" (Tier 5 rows), "Securities as items",
"Fictional companies" (defaults), "Digital Record Keeping", "Components" (Display Screen, Circuit Board), "Information
as progression" (Tier 5 row), milestone M7, open question "Time scale".
Design doc "Done when": bond prices fall when the rate rises; the Records Terminal's net worth equals the sum of
linked holdings in a GameTest.

Goal: the player learns what a loan looks like from the lender's side. A bond pays a fixed coupon and its face value
at maturity; its **price moves opposite to interest rates**, and a company's bond is only as good as the company.
Then Tier 5's capstone, **Digital Record Keeping**, finally shows the player their whole financial position in one
place: net worth, holdings, income and upcoming payments.

Guiding principles (James): fun first but grounded in real investing; information in block screens, not tooltips;
nothing risk-free that pays more than the vault; buy and hold should work.

Existing code to build on: `contracts` (BankAccount, BankParams, Cd), `registry/SecurityRegistry` (serials),
`equities` (companies, quarterly earnings, `Equities`), `dealer/WorldEvents` and the Newsstand, `StockService`
(presenting papers, custody), `Securities` (valuing papers), Portfolio Binder and Safe Deposit Box.

## Decisions (answered by James, 2026-09-24: all recommendations accepted)

1. **Split the milestone?** Recommended: **M7a** = the central rate, Bond Desk, Treasury and Corporate Bonds,
   coupons, defaults, guides and quests. **M7b** = Digital Record Keeping (Records Terminal, Record Link, income
   ledger). M7a is playable without M7b.

2. **A central rate that moves.** Today the vault pays a fixed 0.3% a day and inflation is a fixed 0.1%. Bonds need a
   rate that changes, or their prices never move. Recommended: a **central bank policy rate** (starting at 0.3% a
   day) that the bank reviews **every 7 days** (each quarter): hold, raise or cut by 0.05% a day, drawn from the world
   seed, with a mild pull back toward its long-run level and a range of about 0.15-0.5%. Inflation stays at 0.1%.
   Decisions are **news**: announced on the Newsstand at dawn like other stories, with the same unstated head start
   before the market reprices bonds.

3. **Do bank rates follow it?** Recommended: **yes, for new money**. The vault's daily rate becomes the central rate
   (so it moves with each decision); new CDs are priced off it (today's term premiums on top); loans float with it
   (their current spread on top); **existing CDs keep their fixed rate**, which is itself a lesson in locking in. The
   Interest and Term guides change their wording from fixed numbers to "today's rate".

4. **Treasury Bonds.** Recommended:
   - Issued by the Bond Desk (the central bank's window) in three maturities: **2, 4 and 8 quarters** (14, 28 and 56
     in-game days), face value **$100** a bond, coupon paid **every quarter** at the yield on the day of issue.
     Longer maturities pay a small term premium.
   - **Stackable series**: every bond of the same type, maturity day and coupon is identical (like certificates),
     so a stack of 40 is one slot. They carry "coupons paid through" like dividends, and are presented at the Bond Desk.
   - **Price** = the value of the remaining coupons and face value at today's yield for that maturity. So when the
     central rate rises, existing bonds fall in price, more for longer maturities (duration).
   - The desk **buys and sells at its quoted price with a small spread** (about 0.5%), rather than an NPC auction
     book: bond prices come from the rate model, and a dealer desk is how bonds really trade. Alternative: an
     auction book like the Stock Exchange (more work, similar lesson).

5. **Corporate Bonds.** Recommended: issued by **all six companies**, same maturities, coupon = the Treasury yield plus
   a **credit spread** set by the company's risk (OWL small, RSD and ENCH large) and **widening when its earnings
   fall** (so the price drops before any default). Design doc rule: a company **defaults when its earnings are
   negative two quarters in a row**. Then coupons stop and each bond pays a **recovery of 40%** of face value when
   presented, and the series is marked defaulted (papers show it). The company then carries on (a restructuring);
   its shares take the hit through its lost cash.

6. **Time scale** (design doc open question: a 7-day bond lasts 2h 20m of play). Recommended: that's fine for these
   maturities (14-56 days = about 5-19 hours of play), and it matches the quarter used by companies. No separate
   "market day" setting.

7. **Bond Desk screen.** Recommended tabs: **Buy** (pick Treasury or a company and a maturity; shows yield and price;
   buy N bonds), **Sell** (bonds in your inventory with today's price; sell), **Coupons** (collect, like dividends).
   The Buy tab shows the **yield curve**: today's yield for each maturity, Treasury and each company (information
   as progression: Tier 5's view is rates).

8. **Papers and valuation.** Bonds join `Securities`: the Portfolio Binder and Safe Deposit Box take them, and the
   binder values them at the desk's bid. Serial numbers aren't needed (stackable series), same as shares.

9. **Digital Record Keeping (M7b).** As the design doc describes, recommended as written:
   - **Records Terminal** block with tabs **Overview** (net worth: vault balances + bills and papers in linked
     boxes and blocks at their marks, minus debts; a 30-day net-worth line), **Holdings** (each paper with its mark,
     quantity, location, and profit or loss where a cost basis is known), **Income** (by source over 7 and 30 days:
     Dealer and Floor sales, interest, dividends, coupons, trading gains), **Calendar** (coupons, maturities, earnings
     days, CD maturities, margin checks).
   - **Record Link** tool: right-click a terminal, then a Bank Vault, Safe Deposit Box or Trade Route Crate. 16
     links, 64 blocks, same dimension; a link breaks if a block is moved.
   - Only linked blocks count (papers in an ordinary chest are invisible). Income starts being recorded when the
     node is bought (a per-player ledger).
   - Net worth needs a full valuation, so it also counts a Bill Clip's cash and a binder's papers inside linked
     storage.

10. **Unlocks, costs and components** (design doc): Bond Desk **$25,000**, Digital Record Keeping **$20,000**
    (requires Bank Vault and Safe Deposit Box). Components **Display Screen** ($250, depth 8) and **Circuit Board**
    ($400, depth 8). Proposed blueprints: Bond Desk = 2 Engraved Plates + 1 Lock Mechanism + 4 gold + 8 planks;
    Records Terminal = 1 Display Screen + 1 Circuit Board + 4 iron + 2 Ledger Paper; Record Link = 1 Circuit Board +
    2 copper. Tier 5 gate: half of Tier 4. Balance is deferred with the rest (James); `sim progression` reports it.

11. **Quests and guides.** Recommended:
    - Quests: **Coupon Clipper** (collect a coupon), **Held to Maturity** (redeem a bond at maturity), **Rate Watcher**
      (sell a bond for more than you paid after a rate cut), **Balance Sheet** (M7b: see a net worth of $50,000 on a
      Records Terminal).
    - Guides: **Bonds and Yield** (coupon, face, yield, price), **Interest Rate Risk** (why prices fall when rates
      rise, longer bonds more), **Credit Risk** (spreads, defaults, recovery), **Net Worth** (M7b: assets, debts, cost
      basis, why records matter). Numbers pinned by tests.

12. **Existing guides and sims.** The vault, CD and loan guides quote fixed rates today; with decision 3 they quote
    the starting rate and say it moves. `sim equities` gains bonds (Treasury and corporate returns next to the vault
    and shares), with the target that Treasuries earn about the vault rate, corporates a little more with defaults,
    shares the most.

## What to build (after decisions)

### 1. Core (`exchange-core`, unit tests first)
- `rates/CentralBank`: seeded policy-rate path (quarterly decisions, range, pull), decision news; yield curve by
  maturity (term premium).
- `bonds/Bond` (series terms), `BondMath` (price from yield, accrued coupons, duration), `CreditModel` (spread from a
  company's recent earnings; default rule; recovery).
- Bank rates from the central rate (vault, new CDs, loans); existing CDs unchanged.
- M7b: `records/Ledger` (income by source and day, per account), net worth arithmetic.

### 2. Mod layer
- Items: Treasury Bond and Corporate Bond (series data, coupons paid through), components.
- Bond Desk block, `BondService` (issue, buy/sell at quotes, coupons, maturities, defaults, saves), screen with the
  yield curve.
- Central-bank news on the Newsstand; bank rates follow.
- M7b: Records Terminal, Record Link, links, screen tabs.

### 3. Balance
- `sim equities` (or `sim markets`): Treasuries vs vault vs corporates vs shares; defaults; rate shocks.
- `sim progression` Tier 5 timing.
