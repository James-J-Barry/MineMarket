# M5: The Trading Floor (Order Slips, receipts, NPC traders, Ticker Tape, Price Chart)

Source: `docs/design.md` → progression table (Tier 3), "Item and block catalog" (Tier 3 rows), "Components"
(Clockwork Gear), "Recipe-linked pools" (separate books on the Floor), "Counterparties in single player" (market
maker, noise, fundamentalist, momentum traders), "Information as progression" (Tier 3 row), "Technical architecture"
(`agents` package), milestone M5.
Goal: a second way to trade that behaves like a real market. Players post **limit orders** on paper slips; orders
meet each other and a population of **NPC traders** in the existing `exchange-core` batch auction; prices emerge
from supply and demand around the Dealer's fair value, with **tighter spreads than the Dealer** but real depth
limits. The Ticker Tape prints **price history** as a chart. Design doc "Done when": books stay liquid with no
player; prices track fair value in sim.

Existing code: `exchange-core/.../exchange` (uniform-price batch auction, fully collateralized orders, conservation
tests) is the engine. `MarketService`/`MarketCommands` are an in-memory scaffold with placeholder tickers
(DIAMOND/IRON/WHEAT); M5 replaces them.

## Decisions (answered by James: all recommendations accepted)

1. **Split the milestone?** **M5a** = the Trading Floor (agents, order slips, receipts, custody,
   persistence, its guides and quests). **M5b** = Ticker Tape and Price Chart (price history, chart item, their
   guide). M5a is playable without M5b.
2. **What trades on the Floor?** A **curated list of about 10 commodities** in a new `floor_catalog.csv`:
   wheat, carrot, oak log, coal, iron ingot, **iron block (its own book)**, gold ingot, redstone, emerald, diamond.
   Each book needs its own NPC population, so fewer, busier books beat 50 empty ones. Iron ingot and iron block
   trading separately is deliberate: the design doc says separate pools on the Floor create real arbitrage.
3. **Custody: how goods and money move.** Orders are fully collateralized, so something must be held.
   - **Drop-in, collect-out at the Floor.** To place an order you put the goods (sell) or bills (buy,
     limit x qty) in the Floor with a slip; they're escrowed. Fills and refunds accumulate in your Floor account,
     collected from the Floor's output slots (goods and bills) whenever you visit, like the crate's drawer. Nothing
     is virtual: items leave and re-enter the world physically.
4. **Order Slips.** The doc says "free with the Trading Floor" but also lists Ledger Paper as used for slips.
   - A **Drafting Table blueprint: 1 Ledger Paper -> 8 Order Slips** (about 60 cents an order). One slip is
     consumed per order. That's a small, visible transaction cost, and a reason to place fewer, better orders.
5. **Order types.** **Limit orders good for the day** (cancelled at the next dawn, remainder refunded),
   plus a **"market" button**: a limit at a deliberately bad price, filled immediately or cancelled (IOC). Players
   feel the difference between naming a price and taking whatever the book gives.
6. **Receipts.** One fill per auction would bury players in paper. **One Trade Receipt per order**, issued
   when it finishes (filled, cancelled or expired): item, side, filled quantity, average price, day. Receipts are
   what Tier 5's Records Terminal will use for cost basis; they recycle into Paper at the Drafting Table.
7. **Auction timing.** The scaffold runs one auction per second. **Every 10 seconds** (200 ticks): fast
   enough to feel live, slow enough for NPCs to rebuild the book, and the Floor screen shows "next auction in Ns".
8. **NPC traders and money.** The invariant is that nothing prints unlimited money. Per book: one **market
   maker** (quotes both sides, skews prices to shed inventory, widens when its inventory is large), a crowd of
   **noise traders**, **fundamentalists** (buy below, sell above the Dealer's drifting fair value) and **momentum
   traders**. Every NPC has **finite cash and stock that drift back to a baseline over in-game days**, like the
   Dealer's recovery. So a player dumping 1,000 diamonds walks the price down and exhausts the buyers, and the
   Floor recovers over days. Sizes are tuned in sim so the Floor pays **more than the Dealer for normal amounts
   and less for huge ones**.
9. **Where the Floor sits relative to the Dealer.** Fair value is shared (the Dealer's drifting fair value is the
   fundamentalists' anchor). Tuned so the Floor's typical spread is **about 2-6%** against the Dealer's
   20% (12% licensed). The Floor is the better venue for patient traders; the Dealer stays the "sell it now, any
   amount" venue. No direct Dealer-to-Floor link, so a sim check makes sure there's no risk-free loop between them.
10. **Ticker Tape and Price Chart (M5b).** The doc wants a map-style chart hung in an item frame. A custom map
    renderer is a big, unverifiable-headless job. The Ticker Tape prints a **Price Chart item** for
    1 Ledger Paper + 1 Ink Bottle. It's a **snapshot of the last 7 in-game days** of one Floor book (daily close, high
    and low), shown as a line chart when right-clicked and a small text sparkline in its tooltip. Item-frame display
    is parked for later.
11. **Unlocks, costs and components.** From the doc: Trading Floor **$3,000**, Ticker Tape **$1,500** (Tier 3, gate =
    half of Tier 2), new component **Clockwork Gear** ($40, depth 24, 40% spread). Blueprints: Trading Floor =
    4 Gold Ingots + 2 Clockwork Gears + 8 planks; Ticker Tape = 1 Clockwork Gear + 4 Iron Ingots + 1 Ink Bottle.
    Balance note: Tier 2 already runs to about 10 h against a 5 h target; M5's sim reports Tier 3 against the doc's
    ~10 h, and prices stay as the doc says unless you decide otherwise.
12. **Quests and guides.** The doc defines no Tier 3 quests, so these are new:
    - Quests: **Name Your Price** (get a limit order filled), **Beat the Dealer** (sell something on the Floor for
      more than the Dealer's bid that day), **Two Books** (profit by buying iron blocks and selling the ingots, or
      the reverse, the arbitrage the doc promises), **Read the Tape** (print a Price Chart, M5b).
    - Guides: **Order Books** (Floor), **Limit and Market Orders** (Order Slips), **Liquidity and Market Makers**
      (Floor), **Reading a Chart** (Ticker Tape, M5b). Numbers pinned by tests like every guide so far.
13. **NPCs in the world.** The doc shows NPC traders as villager merchants around the Floor, cosmetic only.
    **Park for later.** The simulation doesn't need them, and they're pure presentation work.
14. **The old scaffold.** Replace `MarketService`/`MarketCommands` (placeholder tickers, virtual
    positions, no persistence) with the real Floor service. Keep dev commands under `/mkt floor ...` (book, run an
    auction now, timeshift), registered only in the development environment like the Dealer's.

## What to build

### 1. Core (`exchange-core`, unit tests first)
- `exchange`: day orders and expiry, cash withdrawal for custody, a text save format for accounts, books and last
  prices, and a daily price history (open, high, low, close, volume) per instrument.
- `agents` (new): `MarketMaker`, `NoiseTrader`, `Fundamentalist`, `MomentumTrader`, each producing orders from the
  book, the last price and a fair value; an `AgentPopulation` per book with finite, mean-reverting budgets; seeded,
  so runs are reproducible.
- `FloorCatalog` from `floor_catalog.csv` (instruments, NPC sizes, the market maker's base spread).

### 2. Mod layer
- **Trading Floor** block (M5a): order form (item, side, quantity, price or Market), place order (1 slip + escrow),
  your open orders with Cancel, the book's best bid/ask and last price, "next auction in Ns", output slots for fills
  and refunds. `FloorService` runs auctions every 200 ticks, the NPC population, dawn expiry, receipts, persistence
  (`floor_state.txt`, `players/<uuid>.floor.txt`), and emits events for the new quests.
- **Order Slip**, **Trade Receipt** items; **Clockwork Gear** component.
- **Ticker Tape** block and **Price Chart** item (M5b).

### 3. Balance
- `sim floor`: 30 in-game days per book with no player (liquidity, spread, tracking error vs fair value), then a
  player selling 64 / 256 / 1,024 at once vs the Dealer, and a check that no buy-here/sell-there loop between the
  Floor and the Dealer makes money.
- `sim progression`: Tier 3 timing.
