# M3: Tier 1 content (Bill Clip, Price Board, Merchant License copy, Trade Route Crate, the Capital)

Source: `docs/design.md` → "Dollars" rules, "Information as progression" (Tier 1 row), "Crafting" (licenses),
"Item and block catalog" (Tier 1 rows), "Counterparties in single player" (the Capital dealer), milestone M3.
Goal: the four Tier 1 unlocks **do something**. The player carries cash in a Bill Clip, watches live quotes on a
Price Board, and ships goods to a second market, the Capital, to profit from the price difference (quest 7).
M2 already registered Bill Clip, Price Board and Trade Route Crate as placeholder items and made the Merchant
License an account perk.

**Status: draft. The decisions below need James's answers before building.** Defaults marked *(rec.)* are what
I'd build if you just say "go".

## Decisions for James

1. **Merchant License copy.** The design doc disagrees with itself: the catalog row says the license works
   "while in inventory", the crafting rules (and M2) say it's an account perk and a physical copy is "for flavor
   or trading".
   - *(rec.)* **Cosmetic copy.** The perk stays on the account. The Almanac reissues a paper copy for
     $5 + 1 Ledger Paper. Holding one does nothing; it's a keepsake that shows the owner's name. Multiplier-free
     and unexploitable.
   - Alternative: the copy grants the perk to whoever holds it (tradeable licenses). More interesting in
     multiplayer, but contradicts "unlocks are never items". Park for M11.
2. **Bill Clip payouts.** The doc says exchanges *pay from* the clip. Should money also go *into* it?
   - *(rec.)* **Both.** Purchases and Almanac payments draw from inventory bills and clips; payouts (sales,
     quest rewards, change) go into the first clip with room, then the inventory. The clip is the "wallet"; the
     Basic Exchange's 2×2 drawer stays for sales so the M1 flow still feels the same.
3. **Price Board prices.** Whose prices does a board show?
   - *(rec.)* The **Basic Exchange's unlicensed** bid and ask (the public quote), plus Normal. Same for everyone
     looking at it, cheap to sync. A small "L" marker isn't worth the complexity.
4. **Price Board refresh.** Live or "last marked" (design open question)?
   - *(rec.)* **Live, every 5 seconds** (100 ticks). Boards are the thing you *bought* to see prices; stale
     values there would feel broken.
5. **Capital prices.** The doc says only "different fair values and inventory".
   - *(rec.)* Same items as the Dealer, fair value × a **group multiplier**: Farm ×1.30, Mobs ×1.20, Wood and
     Stone ×1.10, Mining ×0.90, Components not traded. Own depth (same as the Dealer), own drift (different
     seed), **15% spread**, and the Merchant License does **not** apply (it's a Basic Exchange license).
     Farmers gain most from shipping; miners should keep selling locally. Numbers go in a
     `capital_catalog.csv` so they're tunable without code.
6. **Settlement timing.** "Paid after 1 in-game day": priced when shipped, or on arrival?
   - *(rec.)* **Priced on arrival.** The Capital's price can move during the day (drift, and your own earlier
     shipments still recovering), which is the settlement-risk lesson. The crate shows the Capital's *current*
     quote as an estimate before shipping.
7. **Buying at the Capital.** *(rec.)* **Sell-only** in M3. Buying there and shipping back doubles the scope and
   isn't needed for the arbitrage lesson.
8. **Quest 7 "Two Markets" goal.** *(rec.)* A shipment whose payout, after freight, beats what the local Dealer
   would have paid for the same goods at shipping time. Recorded at ship time as the local quote, compared on
   arrival.
9. **Guides.** The doc says one guide per unlocked node. *(rec.)* Four new guides granted by the Tier 1 nodes:
   **Cash on Hand** (Bill Clip), **Reading a Quote** (Price Board), **Transaction Costs** (Merchant License),
   **Two Markets** (Trade Route Crate). Players who already own a node get its guide on next login.

## What to build

### 1. Bill Clip (item, stack size 1)
- Right-click opens a 9-slot container that accepts **only currency** (shift-click works; other items refused).
  Contents stored on the item (vanilla `CONTAINER` data component; verify with `api`).
- `Wallet.count/pay/give` include every clip in the player's inventory (pay: inventory bills first, then clips;
  give: clips first per decision 2). The Basic Exchange cash readout and the Almanac count clips.
- Losing the clip loses its contents (it's an item). Dropping it, or dying, drops the money with it.
- GameTest: pay $30 from a clip holding a $100 bill with no loose cash; change lands back in the clip.

### 2. Price Board (wall block with a block entity)
- Replaces the placeholder item (same id, so existing items in worlds become the block item).
- Placed on a wall like an item frame, facing out. Four item slots set by right-clicking with an item (sneak +
  right-click empty-handed takes the last one back). Only Dealer-traded items are accepted.
- Face shows each item's icon, **Bid / Ask** and a small arrow vs Normal (▲ above, ▼ below). Renderer is a block
  entity renderer (verify the 26.1 BER API with `api`; can't be checked headlessly).
- Server recomputes quotes every 100 ticks and syncs via the block entity update packet. Items are saved in the
  block entity; breaking the board drops them.
- Crafted at the Drafting Table (already in M2 blueprints).

### 3. Merchant License copy (per decision 1)
- Almanac Upgrades tab: owned Merchant License shows **Reissue copy ($5 + 1 Ledger Paper)**. Gives a paper item
  "Merchant License" with the owner's name in its tooltip. No effect on spreads.
- The license reissue M2 deferred. Ledger Paper becomes visible in the Buy tab once the license is owned.

### 4. The Capital (core, `exchange-core/.../dealer`)
- A second `Dealer` instance with its own catalog (`capital_catalog.csv`, built from the Dealer catalog × group
  multipliers per decision 5), params (15% spread) and seed. Reuse `Dealer` unchanged; add a `CapitalCatalog`
  helper that derives it, with unit tests (multipliers applied, components excluded, linked items follow).
- `Shipment(id, owner UUID, crate pos + dimension, items, localQuoteCents, shippedDay, arrivesDay)` and a
  `ShipmentBook` (pure Java): add, due(day), settle(dealer, day) → payout cents after 5% freight
  (freight rounds up, payouts down to the dime, as ever). Serialized with the Capital's `DealerStateIO` state.
- Unit tests: freight maths, arrival after exactly 1 day, pricing on arrival, a crate shipping twice in a day
  pays the second shipment less (shared Capital inventory).

### 5. Trade Route Crate (block with a block entity and menu)
- Replaces the placeholder item. 9 cargo slots + a 2×2 payout drawer, a **Ship** button, and a status line:
  "Capital pays about $X (local Dealer: $Y), arrives day N". The estimate is the Capital's current quote minus
  freight.
- Ship: moves the cargo into a `Shipment` (items leave the world; they're sold on arrival). One shipment in
  transit per crate at a time.
- On arrival (checked on the day tick), the payout goes into the crate's drawer as bills. If the crate was
  broken, the payout goes to the owner's inventory when next online (keyed by UUID).
- Owner-only: the crate remembers who placed it; other players can't ship from it (multiplayer-ready).
- Emits `ProgressionEvent.Shipment(item mix, localQuoteCents, payoutCents, day)` on arrival (new event type).

### 6. Quests and guides
- Quest 7 goal becomes `ship_beats_local` (decision 8); stays worth $40.
- Four guides (decision 9), 150–250 words, one real-world parallel each, numbers pinned by `GuidesTest` like M2.
  Two Markets example: 128 wheat shipped vs sold locally, with the Capital multiplier.
- `PlayerProgress` re-applies grants of owned nodes on load, so existing players get new guides.

### 7. Balance
- `sim progression` gains an optional Trade Route Crate phase: after Tier 1, ship farm goods daily and report
  income per day with vs without the crate. Target (design): Tier 2 should earn "3–5× more per hour"; Tier 1's
  crate should lift a farm-heavy player's income by roughly 30–50%, not multiply it.
- Record the Capital multipliers and results in `docs/design.md` (new "The Capital" subsection).
